Legal

What the book claims, what it does not, and where the limits are

These provisions apply to this website (willowwhitman.com) and to the book it distributes. The book's own copyright page carries them in full; they are restated here so a reader meets them before opening the book, and so anything quoted from this site travels with them. Effective July 2026 · last updated 10 August 2026.

1 · What the book does not claim

The book is built primarily from publicly available records — court documents, declassified files, official inquiries, peer-reviewed research and published journalism. Where an individual was charged but not convicted, or was acquitted, that fact is stated. Nothing in the book, and nothing on this site, should be read as an assertion of guilt beyond what the cited public record establishes.

Where two cases resemble each other, the book's default assumption is analogy: unrelated actors converging independently on the same small set of methods that work — no contact, no coordination, no link assumed. A documented connection (homology) is asserted only where the evidence forces it, and is labelled as such where it appears. The book's central claim of scale-invariance is a claim about shape, never prevalence: it does not say that this mechanism is everywhere, that most relationships or institutions run it, or that any particular situation involves the whole stack rather than one layer.

Claims are tiered — documented, synthesis, contested — and the tier is stated where the claim is made. Quoting a synthesis or contested claim as if it were established misrepresents the book. Steps were taken to protect victim anonymity; any resemblance to persons or events beyond the public record is unintentional.

The practical steps are not universal

The book's appendices describe steps — documenting, recording, securing a device, moving money — that were lawful and safe for some people and are neither for others. What is legal where you live, and what is safe in your particular situation, are questions neither the book nor this site can answer. Confirm both locally, with an advocate or a lawyer, before acting. The law described in the book was current as at July 2026; nothing in it is a statement of the law where you are, on the day you are reading it.

4 · Privacy

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads no third-party scripts. There is no mailing list, no sign-up and no account. The author does not know, and cannot know, who reads this page.

The site is served by Vercel, whose infrastructure keeps standard server logs (such as IP addresses) to operate and secure the service; those logs belong to Vercel's systems, not to the author. Email sent to the correction address is read by the author and used for nothing else.

If the device you are on may be monitored: the Quick exit button in the corner leaves this site at once, but it cannot erase your browsing history. A private or incognito window, or a device the other person cannot reach, is safer than clearing history afterwards — a suddenly-empty history is itself conspicuous. The same caution applies to email: if your account may be monitored or its password shared, write from one the other person cannot reach.

5 · Corrections

The book asks to be checked rather than believed, and that request is worth nothing unless an error, once demonstrated, is actually fixed. Anyone — including anyone named in the book, or writing on their behalf — who can identify a factual error, a misattributed or superseded source, a development the record has since overtaken, or a claim that outruns the evidence offered for it is invited to say so: willowwhitman@proton.me.

Correspondence to that address is acknowledged within seven days, and every claim of error is checked against the record it cites before it is answered. A correction that survives checking will be made, recorded against the version number, and published on the errata page. Where a matter is genuinely contested, the disagreement will be recorded alongside the claim rather than resolved silently — and that recording is open to the complainant's own stated position, within reason, verbatim. The author's pseudonym does not narrow any of this: a correction is owed to the record, not to the person asking for it.
