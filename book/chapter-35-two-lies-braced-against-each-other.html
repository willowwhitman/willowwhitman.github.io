Part V · Chapter 35

Two Lies, Braced Against Each Other

Two lies, told to two different people and braced so that each makes the other believable: tell a victim she is implicated in a crime so she isolates herself, then tell her family she has been kidnapped — and her genuine unreachability confirms the second.

The false evidence ploy has a criminal cousin — run not by police against a suspect but by transnational crime networks against private individuals, for direct financial extortion, and at a scale the FBI has quantified.

Criminals impersonate Chinese law enforcement, prosecutors, or embassy officials and call targets — predominantly Chinese nationals and international students across the United States, Canada, Australia, and the UK — falsely claiming the victim is implicated in a financial crime: money laundering, fraud, an intercepted package, a document forged in their name.1 The caller threatens arrest, deportation, or violence unless the victim complies with a “fraud investigation.”2 Between December 2017 and February 2019 alone, the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center logged more than three hundred fifty complaints reporting a combined loss exceeding forty million dollars — over a hundred sixty-four thousand dollars per reporting victim, among the highest per-victim losses of any scam category the Bureau tracks.3

The escalation is worse than the financial scale. Once a victim believes they are implicated, criminals sometimes direct them to isolate themselves — checking into a hotel alone, cutting off contact with family — as part of the fake investigation.4 They then contact the family separately, claim the victim has been kidnapped, and demand ransom. No one has been kidnapped.5 The isolation the first lie produced is exactly what makes the second believable: the victim is now genuinely unreachable, a real fact manufactured to make the second lie land. The FBI’s December 2025 advisory records a further step: criminals now send the family AI-generated photos and video of the supposed hostage as fabricated “proof of life,”6 evidence a frightened family has no immediate way to verify.

And that is where it breaks. The structure depends on its two audiences never reaching each other; the manufactured isolation is load-bearing, and it collapses the instant the family reaches the victim through any channel the criminals do not control. Every FBI advisory reduces to one instruction: stop and verify independently — call the person, call the real agency, confirm the arrest or the kidnapping that supposedly happened. Even AI-forged proof of life dies against a single direct phone call.

Every manufactured reality in Part V so far has been the work of one person or a small crew. The chapter that closes it asks what changes when the machine building the false reality is an institution, running for years, and the thing manufactured is an entire criminal case.
