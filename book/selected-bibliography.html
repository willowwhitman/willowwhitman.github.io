Publisher and year for the books and reports have been verified against library and publisher records; full details for periodicals, legal cases, and broadcasts sit at the relevant endnote. Certain primary artifacts discussed in the text — notably the forged Protocols of the Elders of Zion* — are documented, with the context that they are forgeries or propaganda, at their endnotes rather than listed here as sources.*

Further Reading — The Craft of Covert Communication

The chapter on deniable communication (Chapter Forty-One) rests on the sources at its own endnotes. The works below are not cited in the text; they are the standard literature for a reader who wants to understand how hidden communication actually works, and each has been verified against publisher and library records. One distinction the chapter turns on is worth repeating here: almost all documented covert communication is cooperative — a message sent to a witting recipient who already holds the key — which is a different thing from a message aimed at an unwitting target. These are histories of the former.

  • Kahn, David. The Codebreakers: The Comprehensive History of Secret Communication from Ancient Times to the Internet. Rev. ed. Scribner, 1996. — The definitive history of codes, ciphers, and concealed writing.
  • Macrakis, Kristie. Prisoners, Lovers, and Spies: The Story of Invisible Ink from Herodotus to al-Qaeda. Yale University Press, 2014. — The first history of steganography and invisible ink, from antiquity to the digital age.
  • Wallace, Robert, and H. Keith Melton, with Henry R. Schlesinger. Spycraft: The Secret History of the CIA’s Spytechs, from Communism to Al-Qaeda. Dutton, 2008. — Dead drops, signal sites, concealment, and covert agent communication, by a former director of the CIA’s Office of Technical Service.
  • Mendez, Antonio, and Jonna Mendez, with Matt Baglio. The Moscow Rules: The Secret CIA Tactics That Helped America Win the Cold War. PublicAffairs, 2019. — Signaling and communicating while under total, continuous surveillance.
  • Wayner, Peter. Disappearing Cryptography: Information Hiding — Steganography and Watermarking. 3rd ed. Morgan Kaufmann, 2009. — The technical methods for concealing data inside ordinary image, audio, and text files.
  • Cox, Ingemar J., Matthew L. Miller, Jeffrey A. Bloom, Jessica Fridrich, and Ton Kalker. Digital Watermarking and Steganography. 2nd ed. Morgan Kaufmann, 2008. — The academic standard on digital steganography and, as importantly, its detection.
  • Bush, Lewis. Shadows of the State. Brave Books, 2018. — A documentary study of numbers stations, the shortwave broadcasts used to reach agents.
  • Wilson, Timothy. Flags at Sea. Naval Institute Press, 1999. — The history of visual signaling at a distance, and the flag, lamp, and heliograph traditions behind it.
  • Singer, P.W., and Emerson T. Brooking. LikeWar: The Weaponization of Social Media. Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2018. — How signaling, coordination, and influence move through social-media content at scale.
  • Rid, Thomas. Active Measures: The Secret History of Disinformation and Political Warfare. Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2020. — The century-long history of state disinformation and influence operations.
